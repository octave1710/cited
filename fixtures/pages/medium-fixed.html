Vitamin C Serum Guide: Concentration, Timeline, Buying Criteria

Vitamin C serum at 10-20% concentration reduced hyperpigmentation scores by 29% on average within 12 weeks across 31 published trials. This guide covers what it does, how to use it, and the 3 things to check before buying. Last verified July 20, 2026.

What are the benefits of vitamin C serum?

Measured brightening and photoprotection: a 2023 meta-analysis of 31 trials found a mean 29% improvement in hyperpigmentation scores after 12 weeks of daily use. Collagen-related changes become measurable from week 12, which is why dermatologists frame it as a 3-month commitment, not a quick fix.

"Vitamin C is the rare active where the marketing claim and the trial data mostly agree. The catch is that nothing visible happens before week eight," says Dr. Sofia Reyes, board-certified dermatologist at Meridian Skin Lab.

How do you use vitamin C serum?

Apply 3-4 drops to clean skin every morning before moisturizer and SPF. Consistency beats quantity: trials showing the 29% improvement used once-daily application for 12 weeks. Morning use matters because vitamin C neutralizes roughly 45% of the UV free radicals that pass through sunscreen filters.

What should you look for when buying?

Three criteria predict whether a serum keeps its potency: 10-20% L-ascorbic acid (15% is the most common trial concentration), opaque airless packaging, and a pH between 3.0 and 3.5. L-ascorbic acid in clear dropper bottles loses most of its potency within 4 weeks of opening. Sensitive skin does better with derivatives like magnesium ascorbyl phosphate.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use vitamin C with retinol?

Yes, split by time of day: vitamin C in the morning, retinol at night. A 2026 Copenhagen protocol pairing 15% vitamin C with 0.3% retinol measured a 31% wrinkle-depth reduction in 16 weeks.
