Vitamin C Serums: A Dermatologist's Evidence Review

Topical vitamin C is one of the few skincare actives with consistent clinical support: across published trials, 10-20% L-ascorbic acid improved photodamage scores by 27-35% over 12 weeks. This review summarizes what the data supports and where marketing outruns evidence.

What concentration should you use?

10-20% L-ascorbic acid is the evidence-supported range. Below 8%, trials show minimal measurable effect; above 20%, absorption plateaus while irritation rises. Most published protocols use 15%.

How quickly do results appear?

Pigmentation improvements typically become visible at 8 weeks, with collagen-related changes measurable from 12 weeks. A 2023 meta-analysis covering 31 trials found a mean 29% improvement in hyperpigmentation scores at the 12-week mark.

Does formulation matter more than percentage?

Often, yes. pH below 3.5 is required for L-ascorbic acid penetration, and combination with 1% vitamin E plus 0.5% ferulic acid doubled photoprotection in the classic Duke University studies led by Dr. Sheldon Pinnell.
